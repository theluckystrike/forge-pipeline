Executive summary (60 seconds)

Verdict

v1 and v2 built a working contribution engine and pointed it at people with no budget. The revenue is zero by construction: the funnel has a hard zero at the "ask" stage, and only 2 of 26 merges (0.077) landed in a repo whose owner has any budget. Every dollar in the comparable set goes to (a) the owner of something people depend on, (b) a verified bug, or (c) a company paying for maintenance of its own code. Nobody gets paid for drive-by PRs to strangers.

Thesis of v3, the PR is a doorway, not a donation

Keep the engine (cheap, verified, 56 to 66% merge where a maintainer decided, E33). Change three things. 1) Target orgs that pass a buyer-budget qualifier, not repos that merge fast. 2) Sell the same work to the repo owner or deployer as a fixed-fee retainer, where AI-agent PRs merge at 68 to 80% instead of 26%. 3) Move the ask off GitHub, one message per org, after a merge or a measured audit, never in a PR body.

Why the realistic number sits between low and mid

  • Volume is the binding limit. 213 audits gave 9 drafts, and the 533 qualified targets not yet audited would add about 22 more. Reaching 160 sends means about 3,800 audits, so discovery has to widen. That's compute time, not a wall.
  • 5 of the 9 drafts go to maintainers (Strapi, Rocket.Chat, Dify, Infisical, Xberg). The evidence showed 0 of 11 such companies pay for translation because they get it free, and section 5 killed that segment on day 0. The builder still drafts it, so those 5 sit near the low row.
  • The retainer hooks are thin. Three broken doc links is a weak opener for $1,500 a month, and the $900 entry offer is more credible.
  • Only 22 targets have both a named decision-maker and an email address.

What raises the probability most

  1. Warm relationships first. Maintainers who already merged your work (Toss react-simplikit, deja-vu, mcp-gitlab) know your name, and a warm reply rate is several times a cold one.
  2. Volume. Going from 30 to 160 sends moves the mid case from 12.7% to 51.4%.
  3. A smaller first yes. A $50 audit or a $500 fix closes far more often than a monthly retainer, and it can grow into one.
  4. Retarget the 5 maintainer translation pitches to English-only product companies and to deployers who run the product.

How fast the truth shows up

With zero replies after 40 sends, the true reply rate is below 7.5% with 95% confidence (the rule of three, 3 divided by n). With zero after 80, it's below 3.75%, which is the section 5 rewrite trigger. At 8 sends per weekday that's about two weeks.

4. Strategy, the PR as a doorway and not a donation

What stays

  • The ship lane: extract gap, parallel children, en-order merge, issue-first pre-approval, contents-API PUT with byte readback (zero defects on all 10 Medusa PRs, 4,252 keys).
  • The 100/100 gate and HUMANIZE prose rules.
  • Verify artifacts, not prose; incremental-write and resume contracts.
  • ANVIL's maintainer-latency weighting, as one of three factors.

What changes

  • Target selection is gated by a buyer-budget score before a maintainer-latency score.
  • Every PR to a qualified org is followed by exactly one off-platform message to the owner persona (CTO, founder, head of devrel), never to the maintainer inside GitHub.
  • The offer is the owner running our engine on their own repos, with our audit numbers as the hook.
  • The engine also runs on the buyer's private repos after signature, where merge rates are 68 to 80%.

Why owners pay when maintainers do not

  • Maintainers get the same output free from volunteers and Crowdin AI at $1.13 per locale (E25).
  • Owners have a budget line: maintenance retainers trade at $1,750 to $10k/mo (E26).
  • A 40-minute verified PR is worth nothing to a stranger and $125 to 250 to the product company whose docs and locales it fixes.

The audit is the hook

  • Run the drift scanner and locale-parity measure on the target's public repo. Output: "14 numeric claims stale, 3 SDK examples fail, fr locale 39.6% untranslated".
  • That report, one page, is the outbound message body. It is specific, measured, and cannot be produced by a template.
  • The PR that fixes one item is optional proof; the report is the product demo.

6. Targeting model v3

ANVIL v2 scored maintainer latency above stars, which selected repos that merge fast and cannot pay (E9). v3 scores three factors and gates on the first.

score = B x R x L        (each in 0..1; B is a hard gate, B under 0.4 = do not contact)
hard exclusions before scoring: repo on the AI-ban blocklist; owner is a User (not an Organization) for L1/L5;
                                 repo pushed more than 90 days ago

B (buyer budget, gate)            weight   source (gh api unless noted)
  owner is Organization             0.20   repos/{o}/{r}.owner.type
  org has website or isVerified      0.15   graphql organization{websiteUrl isVerified}
  funding or revenue signal          0.25   FUNDING.yml absent AND (pricing page on website OR raised $ in press OR careers page)
  paid tooling in tree               0.15   crowdin.yml / .tx/config / lokalise / sentry / datadog configs
  commits >= 1000 and merged PRs >= 100 0.15 graphql history.totalCount, pullRequests(states:MERGED).totalCount
  disclosure-only AI policy (not ban) 0.10   CONTRIBUTING.md grep: "disclos" present, "ban|not accept" absent

R (reach, who we can address)
  named CTO/founder email findable   0.40   website team page, commit emails on org domain, LinkedIn 1st-degree
  our PR merged or reviewed there    0.30   kpi.db contributions
  measured defect count >= 5         0.30   drift_scraper_v3 + measure.py

L (latency, ANVIL v2 carry-over)
  median external-PR merge < 48 h    0.50   last 30 merged PRs
  maintainer comment velocity        0.30   comments on external PRs / 30 d
  pushed within 30 d                 0.20   pushed_at

Proof-PR rules derived from the full-history audit (E34 to E36)

  • Proof PRs go to repos between 100 and 10,000 stars only; over 10k stars merged 1 of 32 in Sept.
  • Proof PRs stay under 50 lines (28.4% merge, 67.4% when decided, E42) unless the maintainer asked; over 500 lines merged 4 of 113 all time.
  • A proof PR open past 48 h without a review is in the tail (80% of merges land inside 24 h); do not send a second PR to that repo, send the audit email instead.
  • Locale fills over 500 lines are delivered only inside a signed L1 or L2 engagement, never as unsolicited proof.

Weights are priors; Phase 2 fits them on outcomes with 3 columns in kpi.db (replied, signed, paid). Re-weight monthly by logistic regression on at least 100 rows, otherwise keep priors.

Capacity rule

The base case fits the 80 h budget (78.2 h). The aggressive case exceeds it by 60 h and is reachable only by dropping L3 (saves 42.0 h, costs $1,590), trimming L5 to one pack per quarter (saves 8.7 h, costs $2,667) and L2 to two locales (saves 9.3 h, costs $1,000): 22,340 - 1,590 - 2,667 - 1,000 = $17,083 per month at 140.0 - 42.0 - 8.7 - 9.3 = 80.0 h, or $214 per hour. The section 5 L3 rule (stop L3 in any month over 80 logged hours) enforces the first cut automatically, so the aggressive case in practice is $17,083, not $22,340.

Sanity anchors

The May 2026 human-in-loop bounty experiment produced $1,067 per month at 10 h per month. Retainer comparables trade at $1,750 to $10,000 per client per month (3 vendors, price pages fetched 2026-09-24, A6). The base case asks for 3.36 clients at the bottom of that band after 320 specific, measured audits at a 5.6% reply rate and 25% close. The 65% of base revenue that sits in L1 (5,880 / 9,085) rests on that untested close rate; the conservative case, at 2% reply and 15% close, is the floor the first 80 sends will confirm or break.
