01Sixty-second summary

Verdict. v3 would spend its operator hours on the lowest-yield touch that exists. Measured 2025 to 2026 cold email reply rates are 0.45% (Belkins, 7.5M emails) to 3.43% (Instantly and Woodpecker platform averages), and those counts include declines and out-of-office replies. At those rates 160 audit emails give a 7% to 42% chance of one client, not the 51% v3 computed from a LinkedIn reply rate measured on 36 messages and a 25% close rate with no source.

Thesis. The per-touch probability of a paying client is the whole game, because operator hours cap volume at about 80 per month. A warm ask to someone who has merged your work converts 8 to 400 times better per touch than a cold email. The operator has exactly one such relationship at a funded company today: Medusa, where a named DX lead approved 6 of 10 PRs and 5 merged this afternoon. Every 14 documented cases of a contributor being hired or contracted through GitHub share one feature: sustained volume in one repo watched by a founder or lead, 20 to 40 PRs over about 3 months at the fast end.

Three moves. (A) Deepen Medusa: finish the 5 open locale PRs, ship 12 docs and i18n PRs into the DX lead's own domain over 30 days, then make one written ask. (B) Redirect the i18n engine to Vendure, whose CONTRIBUTING invites dashboard translations and whose free partner program counts GitHub PRs automatically, backfills 12 months, and routes leads by that score. (C) Pitch subcontract capacity to the 207 agencies already listed as Medusa, Vendure, Twenty, Directus, Payload, Saleor and Strapi specialists, with the merge record as the credential. Cold CTO audits survive only as a capped background lane.

Kill on day 0. All 60 open awesome-list PRs, the licensing-referral lane (its 15% referral cut is not on either Pangea page), Algora and every other bounty board (all dead or pivoted on 2026-09-24), maintainer succession as a 90-day revenue idea, and the v3 sends to maintainers who get translation for free.

03The arithmetic: per-touch probability beats volume

P(at least one client) = 1 minus (1 minus p) to the power of touches. For small p the number of touches for a 50% chance is 0.693 divided by p, and for 90% it is 2.303 divided by p. What zero replies would tell you. Hanley and Lippman-Hand (JAMA 1983): if none of n trials shows the event, the 95% upper bound on the rate is about 3/n. After 80 cold sends with no client, p is at most 3.75%, which is consistent with both the v3 assumption (1.4%) and the Belkins rate (0.045%). A solo sender cannot learn cold email base rates from their own data inside a quarter. Warm and credentialed touches are learnable in weeks because p is 10 to 100 times larger.

What Medusa says. No hire-from-community statement exists on any Medusa page; the careers page renders no roles; the Experts page says "You are an agency that has done multiple Medusa projects already" (2025-06-02). The ask to Medusa therefore has to be a contract or an introduction, not a job.

07Dead ends, measured

  • Maintainer succession. npm: "We do not transfer package, organization, or username ownership simply because another user wants the name." PyPI PEP 541 needs six weeks of documented contact attempts before a transfer. Tidelift pays "the actual maintainers, not people who say they'll help out" and matches packages by registry publish rights. HeroDevs grants ($2,500 to $15,000) require being the maintainer of an EOL project with "strong community adoption and traction". Of 261 open "looking for maintainers" issues from the last 180 days, 4 are libraries with meaningful downloads and a reachable owner (tomlkit 443.9M downloads a month, angular-split 434k). A 6 to 12 month credential, not 90-day revenue.
  • Funds and fellowships. Sovereign Tech Fellowship closed 2026-04-06 with no next date; Season of Docs "has concluded after six great years"; GSoC excludes "Regular contributors to an open source project"; Outreachy needs "must not have a full-time job"; Prototype Fund requires German residence; GitHub Accelerator shows no cohort after 2024. Open and eligible: NLnet Restack and CodeSupply (individuals, "between 5.000 and 50.000 €", deadline "November 3rd 2026 12:00 CET", EU priority, but "We are not interested in AI-generated projects or proposals"), and Claude for Open Source (operator qualifies on "100 or more pull requests merged into repos you don't own in the last 12 months"; benefit is "6 months of free Claude Max 20x", cost avoidance not revenue).
  • Freelance boards. Upwork, Indeed and WeWorkRemotely block unauthenticated fetches; RemoteOK has no tag page for any of 14 products; the one measurable product board (n8n community Jobs) runs about one demand post every two days, and one post drew 83 freelancer replies in 48 hours with $250 to $500 fixed-price asks. No direct marketplace demand exists for Medusa, Strapi, Directus or Payload specialists that is reachable without login.
  • Company-affiliated issue reporters. Measured across 20 repos and 1,834 open issues: 21% of issues have a company-affiliated author, but only 4% carry a first-person production complaint, and only 20 issues have both. Maintainers label 1% as help wanted. It is a thin lane of about 20 warm-ish touches a quarter, kept as lane E with that supply.
  • Cold audit emails. Retained only as lane F, capped, because at measured reply rates the per-send probability is 0.34% or lower and GitHub's policy forbids "using information from the Service ... for the purposes of sending unsolicited emails to users".

11Guardrails

  • GitHub Acceptable Use, live text. Section 10: "You may not advertise in other Users' Accounts, such as by posting monetized or excessive bulk content in issues." Section 7: "You may not use information from the Service ... for spamming purposes, including for the purposes of sending unsolicited emails to users". Lane C contacts come from the vendor directories and company sites, not from GitHub data. Lane E contacts the reporter at the company address found on the company site, one message, with the fix link, never inside the issue.
  • AI disclosure by repo. Of the 20 repos measured, 0 ban AI PRs; n8n "encourage[s] you to note in the PR if AI tools did a meaningful part of the work"; Appwrite, Dify and Documenso require ownership ("you own the pull request"; Documenso: contributors "throwing AI-generated code over the wall without proper review ... will be blocked"). Formbricks has paused community code PRs. Medusa deprioritizes external PRs by policy; that is why the DX lead's attention is the asset.
  • PR size. Cal.com: "Keep PRs under 500 lines of code changed and under 10 code files modified"; the operator's all-time merge rate over 500 lines is 3.5%. Locale fills over 500 lines only where a maintainer has already merged the same shape (Medusa) or inside a signed engagement.
  • Vendure CLA. CONTRIBUTING references a Contributor License Agreement; sign it before the first PR.
  • No calls offered, no emojis, no em-dashes, HUMANIZE rules, $0 paid APIs, LinkedIn automation stays off, Resend key rotated before any programmatic email. Carried over from v3 unchanged.
  • One ask per relationship. The Medusa ask is sent once. A second message is only a reply.
